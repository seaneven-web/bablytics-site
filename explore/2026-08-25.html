Why this book was built twice

THIS BOOK WAS ASSEMBLED TWICE AND THE FIRST VERSION IS PART OF THE RECORD. At 15:06 the chair built a slate and fourteen seats graded it. The Skeptic returned a conviction of 14 out of 100 and refused to recommend anything — and he was right, because what he had found were defects in the chair's SLATE BUILDER rather than in the leads. Contract selection had NO liquidity filter whatsoever, which produced a TMO put with five contracts of volume all session on a 75% spread, an MRNA call holding 0.07% of its chain's open interest, an INTU put struck exactly on max pain, a META leg priced BELOW its own bid and an NVDA leg priced ABOVE its own ask. This document is that second sitting. The first sitting's audit is filed beside it because it is the reason this one exists.

What the desk had wrong
  1. RANK WAS ALPHABETICAL. The builder sorted the slate by ticker and numbered it 1..20, so the #1 lead was #1 for starting with an A. FIXED: rank is now strictly monotone in the chair's score, and the Skeptic re-derived all twenty scores independently to confirm it.
  2. DIRECTION CONTRADICTED THE RESEARCH. A call/put-ratio and RSI cutoff overrode the scan's own direction on six of twenty leads, while the lead's justification line quoted that same scan. FIXED: direction is the scan's, and matches on 20 of 20.
  3. CONTRACT SELECTION HAD NO LIQUIDITY FILTER. It took the strike nearest spot plus one ATR and used `last` if any trade existed at all. FIXED: 500 open interest, 50 contracts of volume, a 15% maximum spread, priced at the ASK, and rejected outright when breakeven exceeds the chain's own implied move. 245 contracts were rejected for cause on this slate — 204 of them genuinely, and the Skeptic is right that the other 41 are monotonic breakeven arithmetic rather than rigour.
  4. THE RANKING FORMULA DELETED DIVERGENCE. It rewarded names that moved, so a name whose entire interest was that it did NOT move could not rank. FIXED with a divergence term — and the Skeptic immediately found the fix overshot: it is a hardcoded six points, it fires on a +0.31% day, and six of twenty leads exist only because of it. That is now a known defect, not a feature.
  5. THE CHAIR'S BRIEF OVERSTATED THE CRUDE TRADE. It said COP was on the slate BECAUSE of the WTI/XLE divergence. COP's own justification line says nothing about crude — it ranked on the generic divergence rule — and COP is the only energy name in a 45-ticker universe, so no formula could have deleted the sector and none restored it. Withdrawn.
  6. THE CHAIR DATED A BRANCH TO THE WRONG MONTH. Wednesday 08:30 is JULY PCE. An August 25th oil move cannot appear in a July print, and core PCE excludes energy in any case. Withdrawn.
  7. THE CALENDAR MISSED A DATED CATALYST THAT BEARS ON ITS OWN HEADLINE TRADE: the EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report, Wednesdays 10:30 ET, on the agency's own published schedule. Added.
  8. FRIDAY 10:00 IS A WARSH KEYNOTE AND TEN LEGS EXPIRE AT 16:00 THAT DAY — six hours later, with no time value left to absorb it. Neither the room nor the chair mentioned this. Added.
  9. THE GAP TAXONOMY WAS BEING MISREAD, AND THE COLLECTOR NOW REFUSES TO ALLOW IT. `gap_pct` is documented as 'the current print against the prior close', which before the open is a real gap and after the close is simply the session's move. Only 2 of 45 candidates in this packet carried a true opening gap, yet all 45 were being handed OPENING-GAP base rates — a name that opened DOWN 0.39% and rallied to +8.14% was labelled 'above_high' and given the 40%-same-day-fill statistic. The collector now reports is_opening_gap and WITHHOLDS the base rate when it has not earned it.
  10. ONE SKEPTIC FINDING IS REFUTED. He reported that the Timekeeper graded 19 leads rather than 20 and that the aggregate never checked the denominator. Checked: every one of the fourteen seats returned a complete 20-lead ballot, because the grading schema enforces exactly twenty. The denominator is sound and the finding does not stand.
